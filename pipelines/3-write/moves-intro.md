# CARS — Introduction-Writing Guideline

Swales' CARS model describes three rhetorical "moves" that almost every research-article introduction makes. This file is the **operational** form: what to do, when to do it, and how to check the result.

---

## When to Apply

- **Drafting a new introduction** → run M1 → M2 → M3 in order.
- **Revising an existing introduction** → run the §4 checkpoints; a *no* points directly to the move to repair (see §5).
- **Do not apply** to abstracts, method / results / discussion openings, related-work blocks, or paragraph-level arguments. CARS is introduction-only.

---

## The Three Moves

### Move 1 — Establishing a Territory

- **Objective:** after reading M1, the reader accepts that the research sits in a live, consequential area and knows roughly what is already known.
- **Minimum viable instance:** one sentence of centrality *or* one topic generalization *plus* at least one cited prior finding.

**Step 1 — Claiming Centrality.** Ask the discourse community to accept that the research is part of a significant or well-established area.

> "Recently there has been a spate of interest in ..."
>
> "Knowledge of X has great importance for ..."

**Step 2 — Making Topic Generalizations.** State current knowledge, practices, or phenomena.

> "The properties of X are still not completely understood."
>
> "X is a common finding in patients with ..."

**Step 3 — Reviewing Previous Research.** Summarize prior findings and cite relevant studies.

> "Both Johnson and Morgan claim that the biographical facts have been misrepresented."
>
> "Several studies have suggested that ... (Gordon, 2003; Ratzinger, 2009)."

---

### Move 2 — Establishing a Niche

- **Objective:** after reading M2, the reader believes that *a specific, nameable thing* is missing or wrong in prior work — not merely that "more work is needed."
- **Minimum viable instance:** one sentence that names the missing object, the counter-claim, the open question, or the continuation.

**Counter-claiming.**
> "While Jones and Riley believe X method to be accurate, a close examination demonstrates their method to be flawed."

**Indicating a Gap.**
> "While existing studies have clearly established X, they have not addressed Y."

**Question-raising.**
> "While Jones and Morgan have established X, these findings raise a number of questions, including ..."

**Continuing a Tradition.**
> "Earlier studies seemed to suggest X. To verify this finding, more work is urgently needed."

---

### Move 3 — Occupying the Niche

- **Objective:** after reading M3, the reader knows what the paper claims, that the claim is *unexpected-yet-reasonable*, and how the paper is organized.
- **Minimum viable instance:** a purpose statement *and* an announcement of principal findings. Structure statement is recommended but optional.

**Step 1A — Outlining Purposes.**
> "In this article I argue ..."
>
> "The present research tries to clarify ..."

**Step 1B — Announcing Present Research.**
> "This paper describes three separate studies conducted between March 2008 and January 2009."

**Step 2 — Announcing Principal Findings.**
> "The results of the study suggest ..."
>
> "When we examined X, we discovered ..."

**Step 3 — Indicating Structure of the Article.**
> "This paper is structured as follows ..."

---

## Checkpoints

Every item is answerable strictly yes or no. A *no* tells you exactly which move to repair.

1. Does the introduction contain all three moves, in M1 → M2 → M3 order?
2. Does M1 carry a narrative stance (centrality / generalization / specific prior findings) rather than being a citation dump?
3. Is M2 concrete — does it name a specific missing object, counter-claim, or open question, not just "more research is needed"?
4. Is M3 phrased as a contribution (unexpected-yet-reasonable), not a task description ("we ran X, Y, Z")?
5. Does M3 include at least the purpose (Step 1A or 1B) and an announcement of principal findings (Step 2)?
6. Is the M1 → M2 transition visible — is there a sentence that inverts from "what is known" to "what is missing"?
7. Is the M2 → M3 transition visible — does the paper claim the niche it just carved, or does the niche hang open?
8. If M1 cites ≥ 5 works, are they grouped by stance rather than listed chronologically?

---

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Reader signature | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| M1 without M2 | "Everyone knows this. Why am I reading?" | Add an explicit gap (M2) |
| M2 without M1 | "Gap in what? I don't know the field." | Add minimal territory (M1) |
| M3 before M2 | "Solution to what problem?" | State the gap before claiming the niche |
| M1 citation dump | Long list of citations with no argument | Group citations by stance, not chronology |
| Vague M2 | "More research is needed." | Name a specific missing object |
| Task-list M3 | "We ran experiments X, Y, Z." | Rephrase as a contribution claim |
