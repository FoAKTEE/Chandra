## 3. Attack on “A + K now; B threshold-gated”

**(a) A per-ledger flock fixes only part of the concurrency problem.** Separate CLI processes do not defeat a cooperative filesystem lock; they make it necessary. Packets and jobs overlap through `main.ts:187` and `scheduler.ts:97`. However, the default observer runs afterward, and `main.ts` does not call the validator API: it is inaccurate to assume all named roles currently append simultaneously through that loop.

Three additional controlled experiments distinguish the missing contracts:

- Hold P's exclusive lock; let P's gate observe Q's predecessor as solid; acquire Q's independent lock and demote it; finish P's append. Both chains are valid, but P's child is solid on a hypothesis. A fresh append rejects the same dependency. Individual ledger serialization does not serialize the gate's reference read set.
- While holding the writer lock, deliberately flush half a serialized row. The existing unlocked reader fails with `Expecting value: line 1 column 326 (char 325)`; chain verification reports `unparseable line`. Both recover after the remaining bytes are written. This is fault injection demonstrating missing reader coordination, not a claim about the frequency of split short writes.
- A verifier subprocess taking a shared lock succeeds normally but fails under its parent's exclusive lock with `BlockingIOError: [Errno 11] Resource temporarily unavailable`. **Inference:** replacing that nonblocking probe with a blocking acquisition waits until timeout. Holding arbitrary verification across a writer lock conflicts with the obvious shared-reader remedy.

Specify a coherent committed snapshot, crash behavior, lock order, batch visibility, sequence allocation, and deduplication within the transaction. A workable A direction is verification against immutable inputs outside a short commit critical section, followed by read-set/version validation under a repository transaction lock or ordered dependency locks. Blindly rerunning long verifiers while holding locks is insufficient. CSV/index publication must obey the same snapshot contract. An advisory lock does not protect files from a non-cooperating writer.

Call consolidation also needs **before-work and after-all-work epochs**. Reusing one snapshot throughout a mutating wave can falsely show no progress. `ledger.ts:115` currently performs three independently timed reads, and the packet's second snapshot can precede completion of concurrent jobs. The note's “14” is a static subtotal: chain checks, digest cadence, outcome checks, and startup change the number. Define the counted calls and coherence requirements rather than promising one launch per wave.

**(b) `supersedes` plus `retired` needs a data model, not just fields.** Same-id revisions already preserve history. A superseding revision is not necessarily an equivalent substitute: redirecting a proof dependency from “A holds” to “A does not hold” is invalid. Define entity identity, immutable revision identity, explicit equivalence/alias edges, stale/forked targets, cross-paper ownership, and retirement of nodes that still have dependents. An error trial has no stable `entry_id`; its replacement identity is currently unspecified.

The proposed comparison keys omit assumptions, evidence, scope, claim kind, and other scientifically significant fields. In an actual batch, unchanged knowledge status/summary caused changed predecessors **and a failing verifier** to be skipped; an incomplete row was skipped too. Claims skipped changed anchors and `blocking`. These checks precede validation (`knowledge_database.py:242`, `claims_database.py:203`). A `supersedes`-only change can be skipped the same way. Result/error batch retries are not currently idempotent: each replay produced a second row. Specify complete canonical request identity, validation-before-deduplication, partial-batch recovery, and rejection of conflicting retries.

Preserve legitimate promotions: all 14 self-ledger hypothesis→solid transitions leave summaries/predecessors unchanged; the mission fixture likewise promotes existing nodes (`orchestrator/test/mission.test.ts:138`). Keep legacy `amended` interpretation explicitly compatible while adding retirement separately. Status demotions must still invalidate/report support. There are **six literal `amended`-mentioning skills**, not twelve; twelve operational skills are affected by A's combined changes. This is more than wording: existing Verify blocks assert automatic CSV creation, admit an orphan trial, replace predecessors without lineage, and submit free verifier commands. The [compatibility inventory](/tmp/chandra/kb-analysis/refute/knowledge/skill_compatibility.json) names all twelve. Update these executable fixtures and old drafts without rewriting historical hashes.

**(c) Blanket “obligations block readiness” can deadlock the repair itself.** The real validator creates an ownerless, blocking repair on the rejected node (`validator.ts:190`). Jobs only give special treatment to `owner === "0-acquire"` (`jobs.ts:99`). Adding the proposed frontier exclusion, without adding repair execution, produced:

```json
{"verdict":"rejected","repairOwner":null,"repairStatus":"open","currentJobs":["work-packet:packet:P::n"],"proposedJobs":[],"complete":false,"missionLoopBranch":"no_ready_jobs exit 3"}
```

This counterexample tests the proposed predicate, not a complete implementation of A. Separate prerequisite blockers, work needed to discharge a repair, and conditions for final closure. Respect `blocking:false`. Use the same eligibility rule for packet roots and interiors: changing only `readyFrontier` still leased a blocked/retired child through `scheduler.ts:45`.

Global lookup must also retain mission scope. Importing P, Q, and shared rows made `Q::future` ready as P's work; an empty P scheduled Q's packet instead of decomposition. Completion/gate scoping alone does not fix leasing or `jobs.ts:90`'s global map-size check. In the real self mission, the three Stage 0 blocking `kb-*` obligations have **empty `node_ids`** and owner `self-optimize`; a node-only predicate never sees them. Define paper-level blocker ownership and a runnable route to discharge them. Current all-solid completion ignores even attached open repairs.

**(d) B's numeric thresholds are provisional policy choices.** Neither 100000 rows nor 5% is an established crossover; there is no indexed comparison or measured wave-time denominator. History depth, unique entities, row bytes, DAG topology, append rate, retrieval workload, and latency distribution matter. The valid-claims 16 MiB failure occurs at a byte limit irrespective of row count. First measure real missions after eliminating repeated scans, CSV rewrites, and process overhead; distinguish elapsed critical-path cost from summed overlapping I/O.

Deferring B is reasonable on the observed 14-node self workload. Saying B necessarily adds a second implementation of every admission rule is overstated: shared validators can use interchangeable query adapters. Its index must be rebuilt/published against a coherent vector of ledger versions, including newly created/deleted ledgers. Per-ledger locks alone do not define atomic freshness for one global SQLite index. FTS availability also needs a consumer-host check; presence on this host is insufficient.

## 4. Problems omitted or not resolved by the note

The following are additional reproduced scenarios or traced paths, including consequences beyond already recorded `drift-*` findings. “Observed” means a local deterministic test; “traced” means source inspection; recommendations and projected effects are inferences.

1. **Validator review is not bound to the admitted submission (observed).** Candidate `(P, P::n, reviewed claim)` with a proposed result `(Q, Q::unrelated, opposite claim)` was admitted to Q. Another candidate's live `resultRow` was mutated inside the scripted judge callback, after pack construction and refutation; the admitted claim differed from `CLAIM.md`. `validator.ts:155` through its append at line 184 lacks equality/immutability checks. Deep-copy and hash the candidate, bind paper/node/claim/revision, and admit only the reviewed snapshot. Task-command registration alone does not repair this.

2. **A context pack is not an enforced isolation boundary (observed transport behavior; bounded inference).** `validator.ts:114` joins unchecked evidence paths; `../outside-pack.txt` copied outside the pack while `checkIsolation` returned `{"ok":true,"violations":[]}`. Its manifest loop at line 146 checks existence without containment. Through the actual `CodexValidatorRunner` transport, a benign local stub received `danger-full-access`, read an outside sentinel, changed packed content after the isolation audit, and changed repository evidence before successful admission; the judge saw changed packed text. No live model or actual sandbox escape was tested. The validator **defaults to read-only** (`codex.ts:20`); the explicit permissive override (`validator.ts:244`) rests on cwd/prompt, which cannot provide the promised isolation. Require path/realpath containment, role-specific configuration, enforced isolation where promised, and immutable packs with checks between phases and at admission.

3. **Adjudication provenance is disposable and permissive (traced/observed).** `parseJudgeVerdict` accepts `{"verdict":"admit"}` with empty reasons (`validator.ts:64`); journal entries retain truncated findings and omit full judge reasoning (`validator.ts:176`), and the pack is deleted in `finally` at line 201. `main.ts` never invokes `adjudicateCandidate`; the end-to-end fixture inserts it manually (`orchestrator/test/mission.test.ts:110`). The existing skill even asserts absence of production wiring (`.claude/skills/adversarial-validate/SKILL.md:164`). Decide the production contract and retain the review/verdict/input hashes needed to audit it.

4. **Settlement can be semantically invalid at first admission (observed).** Exact-proof claims admitted on conjectural, conditional, approximate, empirical, and existence-only results whose verdict was fail.
